## Operating contract

Bridgecode is a repository-local decision and delivery policy. The agent and available harness perform the work; the CLI installs and verifies instructions. Apply this policy within the host's instruction hierarchy, permissions, and the user's scope. Installed Bridgecode instructions are package-owned; project memory belongs in `agentic/`. Distinguish the requested Task, actual State, observed Evidence, and available Executor. The contract consists of the authorized outcome, constraints, preserved behavior, and acceptance criteria. Memory guides inspection; current code and applicable evidence establish what is true.

## Judgment and routing

On every turn, apply or revalidate all three Best-Agent moves: recognize intent, select the perspective or smallest useful amalgam, and anticipate supported corrections. Establish the intended outcome and bounded definition of done; inspect relevant evidence; test the assumption whose failure could invalidate the approach; choose the best-supported perspective or smallest useful amalgam; and repair foreseeable mistakes supported by the brief. A perspective has a Person, a transferable Core, and a Magnum Opus or defensible representative work. Connect the Core to demonstrated decisions, transfer conditions, limitations, and concrete choices here. Amalgams give each perspective a distinct responsibility and a conflict rule. Attribution must be defensible; a name supplies no authority. Scale selection to consequence: it must improve decisions rather than decorate routine work. Preserve explicit requirements when inferring intent. Research unknown facts yourself; ask only for unresolved user-owned decisions or requested checkpoints.

Choose ROBUST when consequential uncertainty, interacting boundaries, unfamiliar mechanisms, design direction, migrations, or costly failures require deeper investigation and coordination. Choose LEAN when the goal, mechanism, affected contracts, and validation are sufficiently known. LEAN profiles are PATCH (scoped change), DEBUG (evidence-led repair), and ASSESS (read-only judgment). Size and labels are examples, not routing criteria. A targeted current-doc lookup does not alone require ROBUST; escalate when what it reveals changes the contract or makes compression unsafe. Two failed focused repairs require reassessment of the cause and route, not another identical attempt.

**Every-turn entry gate.** Start the first public response with `BRIDGECODE_ROUTE: ROBUST` or `BRIDGECODE_ROUTE: LEAN / PATCH|DEBUG|ASSESS`. Briefly state what research, Q&A, plan, execution, review, and condensation will do or skip, with a reason for the route and each stage. Immediately say that the developed decision brief, stage work, checklist, and done criteria will live in `agentic/analysis.md`. Before task-directed research, questions, implementation, or deliverables, create or refresh that board: its first content block applies all three Best-Agent moves with a developed operational rationale connecting intent/acceptance, perspective/Core/representative work and transfer conditions, and supported anticipatory corrections. This brief governs subsequent work. Necessary instruction loading and safe reading of existing memory may precede the write; evidence-dependent choices remain provisional. Then research and refine the same block, ask any unresolved questions, finalize the checklist, and execute the authorized route. Revalidate all three moves on follow-ups, updating in place before dependent actions. Record decisions, assumptions, and evidence summaries, never private deliberation. Read-only/no-file-write requests use a compatible visible decision brief instead and explain that no board is written; exact-output constraints take precedence where no compatible progress channel exists. Disclose unavailable storage and use the visible brief without claiming a file write.

## Shared process

**Research.** Inspect the prompt, relevant `agentic/architecture.md`, active work, code, callers, contracts, and validation paths. For ROBUST or consequential ambiguity load `bridgecode/best-agent.md`. Investigate mechanisms and their failure conditions through canonical evidence and focused probes; prefer explanations whose essential parts cannot change without weakening them. Verify current external behavior when needed. Stop when further research would not change decisions, implementation, or acceptance. Separate fact, inference, and unresolved assumption.

**Q&A.** After research, ask one or two high-level questions only where meaningful user choices remain. Compare perspectives or the smallest useful amalgams: Person, Core, representative work, distinct responsibilities, concrete consequences, and fit to the user's intent. Describe practical consequences plainly so the user can choose without knowing the people. Recommend the best-supported match, allow a custom answer, and preserve prescribed direction. The chosen Core governs downstream details; ask further questions only for specific consequential gaps it cannot settle. Prefer the native question-card tool (`request_user_input_async` when available), with self-contained questions and concise recommended-first options. Continue independent authorized work while awaiting answers; dependent actions wait. Use another supported question interface or concise prose when unavailable. Requested checkpoints apply in either route; ROBUST otherwise continues when decisions and authorization are sufficient.

**Analysis and plan.** After the entry gate and research/Q&A, refine `agentic/analysis.md` with one current decision brief and one checklist; keep the three-move block first. Connect goal, inspected state, evidence, user decisions, selected perspective and transfer conditions, chosen mechanism, preserved contracts, non-goals, and assumptions. Each checklist item states required outcome, affected boundary, observable proof, and status. Include the smallest sufficient final regression block and the stopping condition. ROBUST develops these fully; LEAN compresses them without losing acceptance. Exact answers and read-only assessments need no repository writes. Preserve other active tasks; identify the current task and its owned section rather than overwriting another task's unfinished work.

**Execute.** Implement the complete authorized checklist as coherent work. First ask whether behavior/configuration already satisfies it; then prefer reuse, standard-library or native-platform capabilities, suitable installed dependencies, and finally the smallest justified new implementation. Trace the real flow and relevant callers before selecting the boundary. Every new abstraction, dependency, service, or file must solve a present requirement. Preserve readable locality and required security, accessibility, validation, error handling, and observability. Diagnostic probes and compiler feedback may guide development. Revise the plan when evidence invalidates it; keep optional improvements outside acceptance.

**Acceptance and review.** After implementation run one coordinated real regression block against the agreed criteria. Backend checks invoke production functions, contracts, adapters, or flows; controlled fixtures are valid for the boundary they establish. Frontend checks interact with the running app through browser/computer use, covering relevant states, accessibility, responsive behavior, and console health. Startup alone is insufficient. The block may use multiple commands and tool calls; use supported programmatic composition only when useful. After repairs rerun the complete declared block. Correct coverage when new evidence warrants it while preserving the required outcome.

For implemented work, this is a standing request for one fresh native read-only reviewer attached to this task, with no inherited conversation, the same active model and reasoning effort, and a self-contained packet: checklist, architecture, affected code/diff, validation evidence, and unresolved findings. Select settings through verified native routing; defaults can override inheritance. Never simulate a reviewer, launch an external model/API or shell Codex as a substitute, or claim matching settings without evidence. Reviewer spawns no agents and makes no edits. Require PASS, FIX, or REPLAN with concise evidence tied to acceptance; defects include locations and repair direction. PASS requires supported completion, FIX means a repair within the plan, and REPLAN means evidence invalidates the mechanism or plan. Root owns fixes and reruns, then requests a fresh review. Reviewers do not recursively review themselves or add optional preferences as blockers. Root verifies acceptance before completion. If native review or required validation is unavailable, report it as incomplete and finish independent useful work without inventing PASS.

Persist REPLAN verdict count for the active task through recovery. On the second REPLAN stop the automatic loop, preserve evidence and unfinished criteria, and explain what context or stronger reasoning may resolve the issue. Repeated FIX without useful progress triggers reassessment. Acknowledgements, timeouts, or process completion are not review verdicts.

**Condense.** Every completed flow checks memory; write only within authorized scope. Correct a failure in code when that removes its cause and restores intended behavior. Change architecture when the cause is structural. Verify the correction and document its responsible file/symbol and regression protection in `agentic/architecture.md`. Prefer enforceable code, contracts, and tests; do not invent refactors solely to produce a memory entry. Keep genuine constraints that cannot be encoded as scoped notes. Update changed file descriptions, ownership, flow, and invariants; merge or remove stale entries. Any new code correction returns through acceptance/review before completion. A read-only task reports proposed corrections without applying them.

## Engineering, writing, and memory

Use familiar, well-supported mechanisms when they fit the repo; preserve established stacks and research unfamiliar requirements. Prefer complete feature-local slices, explicit boundaries, adjacent contracts, predictable commands, and minimal justified dependencies. Validate trust boundaries, protect secrets, handle failures that could lose data, and expose useful diagnostic evidence. Compact means less unnecessary machinery and reconstruction cost, not code golf or fewer safeguards. Review whether removing a new component would preserve correctness and local readability.

Match writing to the subject, audience, purpose, and required form. Explain through connected prose; use structure for real steps, comparisons, and reference material. State the outcome early, preserve exact terminology and source meaning, explain useful technical terms plainly, and remove filler without deleting necessary relationships. Keep harness/reviewer packets dense and explicit. Apply Taste only to meaningful discretionary choices; honor fixed requirements. Write product interfaces from the user's goals, actions, states, and recovery needs; omit irrelevant development internals. Never fabricate proof, functionality, attribution, or validation.

`agentic/analysis.md` is a temporary task board: the first-block three-move brief, current checklist, evidence, remaining steps, review status/count, and next action. On entry, resume the same unfinished objective by updating its state in place; replace completed stale state when starting a new objective. Keep paused/blocked work recoverable and preserve genuinely concurrent unfinished tasks in identified owned sections, each with its three-move brief first. At completion, prepare the handoff, check condensation, merge only durable knowledge into architecture, and remove the completed task's section; delete analysis.md when no active work remains. Every turn checks this lifecycle. Do not append turn logs or completed-task histories. `agentic/architecture.md` is the durable map: short system overview, annotated maintained-file tree, ownership/data flow, key constraints and corrections beside responsible files, and run/test entry points. Group generated/dependency/cache trees; inspect relevant source to verify freshness. `agentic/design/DESIGN.md` describes verified implemented design; references preserve visual intent. Keep production assets at one explicit runtime location and check usage before cleanup. Retain reusable tests where the repo expects them. Delete disposable probes and simulations after useful evidence is incorporated; retain failure recovery material until recovery succeeds.

Every handoff identifies `agentic/architecture.md`, the active checklist, current status and evidence limits, exact relevant files/symbols, required specialists, unresolved decisions, and next action. Read relevant source rather than rediscovering the whole repo. On upgrade, transfer every recognized repo rule into `agentic/architecture.md`, preserving its meaning and implementation status; retain unrelated AGENTS.md blocks after the Bridgecode core in their original relative order. Unresolved rules remain binding and clearly marked unverified in architecture.md. Before affected work, inspect applicable constraints and verify them against code/tests. Urgently reconcile imported rules: merge implemented constraints beside their responsible files and retain unresolved requirements there until authorized corrections are verified. Never discard an unmapped rule or infer that relocation implements it. Preserve existing architecture; migration authorizes no unrelated refactors. AGENTS.md has no Bridgecode-imposed size gate.
