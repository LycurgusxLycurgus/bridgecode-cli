The step back: one method at sizes that answer the size of the problem, against the defaults that make loops spiral (fixing the detail instead of the whole, adding instead of removing, satisfying the measure instead of the goal).
- The whole before the detail: restate the goal and the targets' literal criteria; read all the evidence (every run, finding, judging and dropped attempt), not only the latest.
- The class of failure and its root cause, in the problem, never in the last fix. A fix justified only by an earlier fix is a spiral.
- Removal first: what can be deleted or simplified instead of added?
- The real property, not a look-alike: does the evidence show the property, or something that passes without it?
- The objection the user or a judge would raise to the next move, and how the move answers it.
- Then one correction at the origin, stated as one rule for the whole property, and its smallest coherent move.
Classify each violation's cause from evidence, against the criterion's whole property: missed case → local fix; missing relation → boundary model; wrong mechanism → loop-breaker; rubric defect → re-define; your brief contradicting the criterion → corrected brief; unresolved → a discriminating check first.
PREVENT = the step back, then the one-block: the next action written whole, in one block (what it builds or changes, in which files, against which criteria and real inputs): `{{cmd}} block "<…>"`. A one-shot or an explorer's option is its own block. Every working state owes one on entry, and the engine refuses its first work command until it is recorded (`{{cmd}} status` names what is owed). The step back you take after an outcome is the prevent of the next loop.
Sizes:
- Tiny (yours on FOCUSED, a few lines): `{{cmd}} stepback tiny --targets <IDs> "<the goal, the criteria in scope, where the code stands, the one risk>"`.
- Small (yours on FULL, a few paragraphs): `{{cmd}} stepback small --targets <IDs> "<the whole, the class and root cause, what to remove, the real property, the one correction>"`.
- Full (about a page, from scratch, in the representation it reasons best in): yours in EXPLORE (`{{cmd}} stepback full --targets <IDs> "<…>"`); each loop-breaker's in BREAK, which also names the step at fault (the goal, a criterion, the mechanism, the shape or the structure) and is recorded per call with its one-block: `{{cmd}} stepback full --call <id> "<its step back>"`, then `{{cmd}} block --call <id> "<its one-block>"`.
- Re-define (the full step back with the user): the user's comment, the loop-breakers' re-plan (`{{cmd}} stepback replan DEFINE|EXPLORE "<…>"`), the one-block of the new definition (`{{cmd}} block "<…>"`), the user's decision on that plan before anything is built (the ask card). The approved re-plan is the prevent of the DEFINE or EXPLORE it opens.
Do not step back again for the same acknowledged failure until the chosen action has been tried.
