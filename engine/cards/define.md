Settle what done means before anything is built. The rubric is the finish line; it changes only here or in a re-define.
1. Brief: intent, completion criterion, interpretation and its consequential assumptions, the perspective whose principles fit, the objection to anticipate (compressed on FOCUSED, full on FULL): `{{cmd}} brief "<the brief>"`.
2. Goals with the user: the objective, real inputs, stakes, exclusions and what done means. Ask only the user's decisions, as a table (decision | recommended default | alternative | current behaviour from a run): `{{cmd}} question "<the table>"`, then `{{cmd}} answer "<their words>"` for each.
3. PREVENT, then the draft: your step back on the goals (`{{cmd}} stepback tiny|small --targets <IDs> "<…>"`, the size the engine names); a planner drafts when one is assigned (`{{cmd}} call planner`; several draft apart and you merge); then the one-block: the rubric, and on a new project `spec.md` and `architecture.md`, drafted together as one block (`{{cmd}} block "<…>"`), conjectures that real runs will test. The files are split from it.
4. Merge treatments (the merge card), by user consequence, not by strictness: ELEVATE (CORE: removing it fails the objective, many depend on it, or its failure is severe or irreversible); SPLIT (one property per criterion; "and", several conditions or half-meetable → split); MERGE (duplicates and symptoms of one cause); REMOVE (fails the trace test, or a process criterion); DEFER (valid but supplementary → Backlog); DROP (neither → the Drop list with its reason, never judged again; sub-products such as documents about the code are mostly Backlog or Drop); DECIDE (a required criterion's evidence is a run or a deterministic check giving every judge the same verdict; otherwise make it decidable or move it out); LAW (a universal property, "never", "every", "zero", is tagged `law` and names its check method, strongest first: formal law, runtime invariant, property-based check, real-input oracle, examples); RESOLVE (two readings → fix the wording, or build what satisfies both when both are required, or ask); VETO (mechanisms known to fail). Everything else required is NON-CORE. If an obviously wrong output would still meet every criterion, add the criterion that rejects it.
5. Format, in `agentic/rubric.md` under `## Goals`: `### G1 <objective>`, then `- G1.C1 | CORE | tags: ui | <product part> <does/states> <property> for <inputs in scope>, seen in <evidence> | scope: … | unsupported: … | evidence: …` (tags optional: ui, prose, copy, image, taste, llm, prompt, law). Not allowed: process criteria, bundles, terms with two readings, outcomes over undeclared inputs, undecidable criteria.
6. Pre-freeze check, for every new or amended criterion: run its literal wording against real cases (legitimate uses that must pass, known violations that must fail, the protected tests). A legitimate use it forbids, a violation it admits or a term with two readings is fixed now.
7. Plan review (FULL on existing code; the engine requires it): `{{cmd}} call reviewer` with the rubric and the mechanism against the code; `{{cmd}} planreview <id> "<amendments | none>"`; one amendment pass, every amended criterion through the pre-freeze check again.
8. Freeze: `{{cmd}} freeze`. Then build: FOCUSED, `{{cmd}} advance LOOP` (tiny loops); FULL, `{{cmd}} advance ONESHOT`, or `{{cmd}} advance EXPLORE` when the shape or mechanism is unclear. After a re-define with every required criterion established, `{{cmd}} advance GATE`.
